Completing the Compare Search Engines activity helped me understand that different search engines can produce different results even when the same search terms are used. I learned that search engines use different algorithms and ranking methods to determine which results appear first. I also learned that a useful search engine is not necessarily the one with the most results; the relevance and quality of the results are more important. I can apply this knowledge in my current and future environment by comparing results from multiple search engines when researching important topics instead of relying on only one source. This can help me find more accurate and useful information for school, work, and everyday decisions. The Use Search Engines activity taught me how search operators can make online research much more precise. In particular, using quotation marks to search for an exact phrase can narrow the results to pages containing those specific words together. I also learned that the site: operator can limit results to a particular website or domain. These techniques can save time because they reduce unrelated results. I can apply this knowledge when completing research assignments or looking for specific information at work. Being able to narrow a search quickly will make it easier to locate reliable information without having to look through large numbers of unrelated webpages. The Evaluate Search Results activity taught me that finding information online is not the same as finding reliable information. I learned that credibility depends on factors such as the authority of the source, the accuracy of the information, the date it was published or updated, and the purpose of the website. Comparing information across multiple sources can also help identify inaccurate or misleading claims. I can apply this concept whenever I encounter information online, especially when researching academic, professional, financial, or technical topics. Evaluating sources carefully will help me make better-informed decisions rather than automatically trusting the first result returned by a search engine. The Learn About Artificial Intelligence (AI) activity expanded my understanding of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and large language models. I learned that AI is a broad field, while machine learning is an approach that allows computers to learn patterns from data. I also learned that large language models have limitations and can sometimes produce incorrect information, so their responses should be evaluated rather than automatically accepted. I can apply this knowledge in school and future professional environments by using AI as a helpful tool while still verifying important information and maintaining critical thinking. This will allow me to benefit from AI while understanding its limitations.
